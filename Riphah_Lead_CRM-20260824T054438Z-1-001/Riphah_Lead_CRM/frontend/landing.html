What it does

A sales desk, not a database

Six things a property sales manager actually acts on. Everything else stayed out.

🎯

Tiered on arrival

Every lead scores hot, warm or cold from what the visitor actually said — budget, timeline, project, intent. Spam is quarantined before it reaches the queue.

🔗

Deduplicated across sources

The same buyer enquiring twice — once by chat, once from an ad — is one person, matched on a cross-source identity key rather than an exact email.

⏱

Response targets that surface

Hot leads carry a clock. Anything past its target appears at the top of the dashboard by name, not buried in a report nobody opens.

👤

Ownership

Assign a consultant, see their load, and find every actionable lead that still has nobody's name on it.

📊

Where the good leads come from

Hot rate and conversion by source and campaign — so budget follows the channel that produces buyers, not the one that produces volume.

📤

Yours to leave

Every lead and field exports to CSV. The integration is a documented API, so moving to Salesforce or Zoho later costs a mapping, not a migration.

Analytics

The numbers you run the desk on

Not a reporting suite. Six views that answer where leads come from, which ones are worth calling first, and where the pipeline is leaking.

    How it works

    Enquiry to decision, in four moves

    The assistant captures. This tracks. Neither knows more about the other than an API contract.

    1

    Capture

    A visitor talks to the assistant, by text or voice. Fields are taken from the conversation — never a form.

    2

    Deliver

    A signed webhook fires on create and update, with a pull reconciliation behind it so a missed delivery self-heals.

    3

    Resolve

    Normalised, deduplicated and tiered. Idempotent on source and external id, so a replayed webhook changes nothing.

    4

    Work

    Assign, call, note, convert. Response time and outcome are recorded against the lead, not a spreadsheet.
