## What the project is

Riphah Medical City is a mixed-use medical district planned around a teaching
hospital and its supporting clinical services. The development is aimed at
practising clinicians who want owned consulting space near a hospital, and at
investors seeking tenants in a sector with long lease cycles.

The site sits within the Islamabad Capital Territory, on the corridor between
the I-14 and Gulberg Green campuses. Access is via the main arterial road, with
the hospital block as the anchor tenant.

## Who it is designed for

- **Practising doctors and dentists** who currently rent consulting rooms and want
  to own their practice premises.
- **Diagnostic and pharmacy operators** looking for ground-floor units with
  patient footfall.
- **Investors** — typically overseas Pakistanis — who want a leaseable asset in a
  sector where tenants stay for years rather than months.

## Unit types

### Medical suites

Consulting-room units on the upper floors of the clinical blocks. Configured as a
reception area, one or two consulting rooms, and a washroom. Sizes range from
compact single-practitioner suites to larger units suitable for a small group
practice or a day-care procedure room.

Suites are sold shell-and-core. Fit-out is the owner's responsibility and is
subject to the development's medical fit-out guidelines, which cover waste
handling, plumbing loads and fire compartmentation.

### Commercial ground floor

Retail and service units at street level: pharmacy, laboratory collection points,
optical, cafeteria. These carry the highest footfall in the development and are
the first units to be released in each block.

### Offices

Non-clinical administrative floors intended for practice management, insurance
liaison, billing, and medical equipment suppliers. Fitted to standard commercial
specification rather than clinical specification.

### Apartments

A residential component intended for resident doctors, visiting consultants and
hospital staff, in one- and two-bedroom configurations. Short walking distance
from the clinical blocks.

## Why the location matters commercially

Medical tenancy is unusually sticky. A consultant who builds a patient list at an
address does not move it lightly, and diagnostic operators invest heavily in
fixed equipment. That produces longer average tenancies and lower vacancy churn
than general commercial property, which is the core of the investment case for
the ground-floor and suite inventory.

## Ownership and documentation

Units are sold on a registered-title basis. The buyer receives an allotment
letter on booking, and title transfer follows on completion of payment and
handover, subject to the development's transfer procedure and the applicable
authority approvals.

Transfer of an allotment before completion is permitted under the development's
resale procedure, which carries a transfer fee and requires the outstanding
balance to be current.
